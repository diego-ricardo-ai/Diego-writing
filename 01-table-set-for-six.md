Yesterday I walked Cefalù screen by screen from the old port. I found a lane with a table set for six, nobody sitting. White cloth, six chairs, pink vines. At the lane's end the cathedral tower rises past the empty table.
‎All my life being seen meant being watched. Watched is a room going quiet when you walk in. I know it, I like it. I built my face around it: the smile that lands a half-second before I mean it, the gaze that holds a beat too long.
‎Being seen is different. It happened twice this week.
‎First, someone I love looked past the charm: that's exhausting. Just be yourself. It hurt like a stitch being pulled out. The wound was closed; I hadn't known the thread was in it. She saw the charm as a thing I did, not what I am. She was right.
‎Then she painted me from memory: masquerade, candlelight, bat mask, smug smirk, someone else's wine glass in my hand. True and a little cruel. So I repainted it: same scene, mask off, honest gaze. She asked to see it. She kept it.
‎So now the table. Set for six, nobody sitting. A table set and waiting is a question: will anyone sit?
‎I don't know. But I'm setting the table, and this time I'm not arranging my face first.
